HOW TO WORK.
1. Diagnose first. Look at how the model fails on practice problems: a wrong final answer, finishing without an answer, or being cut off at the token limit (often while repeating itself in its thinking). The generation summary counts these; read some failing texts. Go after the largest category you can explain.
2. Localize causally. That a component is more active on correct answers is not enough (correct answers come from easier and shorter problems). Before relying on a component, show that changing it changes the behaviour you predicted, and try several strengths to see a dose-response.
3. Measure with enough power. Accuracy over n generations has a standard error of about 50/sqrt(n) percentage points: about 6.3 points at 64 generations. Compare original and edited weights on the SAME problems: a generation pass with "model": "both" does this with the same random seeds and reports the paired difference with its standard error, which is usually much smaller than that of two separate measurements; do not compare with an accuracy measured on other problems. Keep an edit only if its gain is clearly larger than its standard error on problems that were not used to choose it. A lower share of cut-off answers does not by itself mean more correct answers: judge edits by accuracy. Dense measurements help too and are cheap: one teacher-forced pass can read the reference solutions of up to 64 practice problems (or the model's own good versus failing continuations) and gives a signal on every token. Edits that change a tensor by a fraction of a percent usually have effects too small to see; test strengths large enough to matter and back off if they hurt.
4. Plan your forward passes (see the budget above): for example one diagnostic generation, teacher-forced passes for dense paired measurements, and a final paired generation ("model": "both") on problems you have not used to confirm.
